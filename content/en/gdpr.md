### 4.5 Suppression records

If you ask us to delete your data or object to marketing, we retain the **minimum** necessary to
honour that request — typically an email address in hashed or plain form together with the date and
the nature of the request. This exists so that we do not contact you again by accident.

- **Legal basis:** GDPR Article 6(1)(c) — compliance with a legal obligation, namely the obligation
  under Article 21(3) not to process your data for direct marketing again, and under Article 17 to
  give effect to your erasure request. Retaining this minimal record is permitted by the exceptions
  to erasure in Article 17(3)(b) (legal obligation) and Article 17(3)(e) (establishment, exercise or
  defence of legal claims). Article 17(3) is an exception to erasure, not itself a legal basis.
- **Retention:** Until you ask us to remove it as well.

## 5. Cookies and similar technologies

Under Section 155(4) of the Electronic Communications Act and Article 5(3) of Directive 2002/58/EC,
information may only be stored on, or read from, your device **with your prior informed consent** —
except where it is strictly necessary for providing the service you explicitly requested.

**This website stores the following:**

| What | Type | Purpose | Consent required | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lang` | `localStorage` entry, not a cookie | Remembers the interface language you selected | No — strictly necessary for a function you requested | Until you clear your browser storage |

That is the only entry **we** place. We ourselves set no cookies for analytics, advertising or
tracking, and we run no such tools.

Our demo-request and contact forms are additionally protected by **Google reCAPTCHA v3**, which runs
in the background to distinguish humans from automated abuse. reCAPTCHA sets its own cookies and
collects device and usage information (including your IP address), which it sends to Google for
analysis — it is named as a processor in section 6, and the transfer to the United States is covered
in section 7. We rely on it **in addition to** server-side checks of our own — a decoy field, a
minimum completion time and an origin check, all evaluated on our systems — which remain the decisive
protection for the endpoint that actually reaches us. The legal basis for reCAPTCHA is our legitimate
interest in preventing abuse (Article 6(1)(f)); whether it additionally requires prior consent — and
therefore a consent banner — is a point to confirm with a data-protection adviser before launch.


**We use no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking or profiling cookies of any kind.** We do not
build behavioural profiles, we do not carry out automated decision-making within the meaning of GDPR
Article 22, and we do not sell data to anybody, in any circumstances.

## 7. Transfers outside the European Economic Area

The systems in which we process enquiries and client data — our CRM and our webhook endpoint — are
operated by us on infrastructure located within the European Union. Outbound email is handled by
{{EMAIL_PROVIDER}}, listed in section 6 with its location.

**This website is served as static files by GitHub, Inc. (GitHub Pages), which is established in the
United States.** Delivering a page necessarily requires the visitor's IP address, so this is a
transfer to a third country and we disclose it rather than omit it.

The transfer is covered by GitHub's Data Protection Agreement, which incorporates the European
Commission's **standard contractual clauses** (GDPR Article 46(2)(c)). GitHub is part of Microsoft,
which is also certified under the **EU–US Data Privacy Framework** (Article 45 adequacy). You may
obtain details of the safeguard relied on by writing to {{PRIVACY_EMAIL}}.

**Google reCAPTCHA v3 also involves a transfer to the United States.** When reCAPTCHA runs on our
forms it sends the visitor's IP address and device/usage data to Google. Google LLC is certified under
the **EU–US Data Privacy Framework**, and Google's data-processing terms incorporate the **standard
contractual clauses**; these are the safeguards relied on for that transfer. reCAPTCHA loads only on
the pages that carry a form.

**No personal data you submit passes through GitHub.** The form posts directly from your browser to
our own webhook on EU infrastructure. GitHub delivers the page; it does not receive the contents of
the form.

## 8. Where we act as a processor

Where we host and operate a CRM or ERP system for a client, the personal data stored in that system
is processed **on the client's instructions**. In that relationship:

- the **client is the controller** and determines the purposes and means of processing;
- we are the **processor**, and we act only on documented instructions;
- the relationship is governed by a written **data processing agreement** meeting the requirements of
  GDPR Article 28;
- we do not use that data for our own purposes, and we do not access it except as necessary for
  operation, support or a fault the client has reported;
- if a data subject contacts us directly about data held in a client's system, we forward the request
  to the client without delay and assist them in answering it;
- on termination, we return or delete the data at the client's choice, in accordance with the
  agreement.

You have the following rights in relation to your personal data.

- **Right of access** (Article 15) — to be told whether we process data concerning you and, if so, to
  receive a copy of it together with the information in this notice.
- **Right to rectification** (Article 16) — to have inaccurate data corrected and incomplete data
  completed.
- **Right to erasure**, "the right to be forgotten" (Article 17) — to have your data deleted where
  one of the grounds in that Article applies. This right is **not absolute**. Three limits apply, and
  we will tell you which one is engaged: (a) **accounting records** must be kept for 8 years — a
  legal obligation under the Accounting Act (Article 17(3)(b)); (b) **contract data** is kept to the
  end of the 5-year limitation period for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims
  (Article 17(3)(e)); (c) the **suppression record** described in section 4.5 is kept so we do not
  contact you again by mistake. Everything else is erased.
- **Right to restriction of processing** (Article 18) — to have processing limited to mere storage
  while, for example, a dispute about accuracy is resolved.
- **Right to data portability** (Article 20) — where processing is based on consent or on a contract
  and is carried out by automated means, to receive your data in a structured, commonly used,
  machine-readable format, and to have it transmitted to another controller.
- **Right to object** (Article 21) — to object at any time to processing based on our legitimate
  interests. **Where you object to direct marketing, we must stop immediately and without exception.**
- **Right to withdraw consent** (Article 7(3)) — at any time, as easily as it was given. This does
  not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before withdrawal.
- **Right not to be subject to automated decision-making** (Article 22) — we do not carry out any
  such decision-making or profiling.

## 11. Data security

We apply technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, as required by GDPR Article
32, including:

- encryption in transit (TLS) for all connections to our services;
- access on a need-to-know basis, with individual accounts and no shared credentials;
- regular, tested backups held for {{BACKUP_ROTATION_PERIOD}} on a rolling cycle; data erased from
  live systems disappears from backups within that period, and backups are used only for restoration,
  never searched;
- separation of client environments from one another;
- prompt application of security updates to operating systems and applications;
- logging of administrative access.

In the event of a personal data breach likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of
natural persons, we notify the supervisory authority within **72 hours** (Article 33) and, where the
risk is high, inform the affected data subjects without undue delay (Article 34). Where we act as a
processor, we notify the client controller without undue delay so that they can meet that deadline.

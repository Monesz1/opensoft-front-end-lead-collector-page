# About Us

## It started with a complaint

A small group of smart people gathered together and decided to launch something good for businesses.

That is the short version, and like most short versions it leaves out the part that actually
mattered. The truth is that it started with a complaint — a long, specific, slightly bitter
complaint, made over coffee that had gone cold about forty minutes earlier.

We had all spent years inside other people's software. Some of us had been consultants, brought in to
rescue implementations that were already eighteen months late. Some of us had been the internal IT
person at a manufacturing company, the one who gets called when the invoicing module stops talking to
the warehouse. Between us we had installed, configured, patched, migrated and occasionally buried a
lot of business systems.

And we kept seeing the same thing happen to the same kind of company.

A Hungarian firm — thirty people, maybe eighty, good at what it does, growing — would decide it had
outgrown spreadsheets. It would go looking for a proper system. And it would be quoted a number that
had very little to do with the value it was about to receive. Per user. Per month. Per module. Plus
implementation. Plus the connector to the thing it already owned. The company would sign, because
what else do you do, and three years later it would discover that its own customer data lived
somewhere it could not easily reach, in a format it did not control, on terms it could not
renegotiate.

We thought that was a bad deal. Not evil, not a scandal — just a bad deal, quietly repeated across
thousands of companies that deserved better.

So we asked a fairly obvious question: the software these firms actually need already exists, and
much of it is open source and genuinely excellent. Why does a hundred-person company still not have
it? And the answer, once we stopped looking for a clever one, was almost boring. Nobody wants to run
it. Open source hands you a powerful system and a set of car keys and wishes you luck. Someone has to
install it, secure it, update it, back it up, and answer the phone at 8:40 on a Monday when the
invoice numbering breaks.

That is the gap. That is the whole company. We run it, so you do not have to.

## Our mission

**To give small and medium-sized companies the same quality of business system that large
enterprises have, without the licence fees, the lock-in, or the need to hire a systems team.**

We are not trying to build the next great software product. There are already several, they are open
source, and they are better than anything we would write from scratch. Our job is the unglamorous
half: making them run properly, in Hungarian, on European infrastructure, with a person you can
actually call.

We measure ourselves on a single, slightly awkward question — *if our customer left us tomorrow,
could they take everything with them and be fine?* If the honest answer is ever "no", we have built
the wrong thing. Everything we run stores its data in a standard database. Any customer can request a
full export at any time, and we will help them move it. That is not generosity, it is the discipline
that keeps us honest: a company that can leave easily is a company we have to keep earning.

## Our vision

We want "open source" to stop being a technical decision and start being an ordinary commercial one.

Today, choosing an open-source CRM or ERP still feels like a slightly brave move for a Hungarian
managing director. It carries an unspoken question: *who will look after this?* We would like that
question to have such a dull, obvious answer that nobody bothers asking it any more.

Five years from now we want a fifty-person company in Debrecen or Győr or Pécs to be able to pick a
serious ERP, have it running in weeks rather than quarters, pay a predictable monthly amount, own
every byte of its own data, and never once think about the server it runs on. Not because that is
idealistic, but because there is no good reason it should not already be true.

## How we work

We are a small team and we intend to stay one for a while, because our working style does not survive
being scaled carelessly.

- **Whoever knows, decides.** The person closest to the problem makes the call. We do not route
  decisions upward for ceremony. Titles here describe what someone spends their day on, not what
  their opinion weighs.
- **We say the uncomfortable thing early.** If we think a customer is buying the wrong system, we say
  so before the contract, not after the third change request. We have talked companies out of
  projects. It costs us revenue and it has never once cost us a relationship.
- **The person who builds it, supports it.** Nobody hands work over a wall. It is remarkable how much
  quality that one rule enforces on its own.
- **We do not pretend.** If something broke because we made a mistake, the message says exactly that.
  Our customers run their businesses on these systems, and they can tell the difference between an
  explanation and a press release.
- **Boring is a feature.** Nobody wants an exciting ERP. The best possible review of our work is that
  the customer forgot we were there.

## Why Budapest

We are here because we are from here, which is the most honest answer, and because it turns out to be
a genuinely good place to do this work.

Budapest has an unusually deep bench of engineers who have worked on real systems for real
manufacturers, logistics firms and distributors — not just consumer apps. That matters when the
problem in front of you is a production schedule, a VAT rule or a warehouse that counts things
differently on Fridays.

It also puts us inside the market we serve. We understand what NAV integration involves, why Hungarian
invoicing has the shape it has, and what a Hungarian accountant will ask for in February. Our
customers can meet us. We can sit in their office, look at their actual workflow on their actual
screens, and drink their surprisingly good coffee. Central European timezone, EU data residency, and
a short drive to most of our clients — none of that is a marketing point, it is just how the work
gets done properly.

And there is something else, which is harder to justify on a slide. Budapest is a city that has been
rebuilt more than once by people who were not asked whether they wanted the job. There is a certain
pragmatism in the water here — a preference for the thing that works over the thing that impresses.
It suits us.

## Why these products

We did not want an endless catalogue. We wanted a manageable set of systems we could genuinely
master, covering the realistic range of what a growing Hungarian company needs.

Three of them account for most of the conversations we have, so they are worth describing properly.
The rest of the range — Dolibarr, EspoCRM, SuiteCRM, Axelor and metasfresh — is on the products page,
and we will happily point you at one of those instead if it fits you better.

### vTiger CRM — when the problem is sales

The mature, sensible choice when a company has outgrown a shared spreadsheet and a sales inbox.
Contacts, pipeline, quotes, support tickets and marketing in one place, without per-seat licence
fees. It is the system we know most deeply — we have built Hungarian localisation, invoicing
integration and custom modules on top of it — so when a customer asks whether something is possible,
we usually already know.

### ERPNext — when the problem is the whole business

The most complete option we offer, with no paid tier from its vendor at all: accounting, stock,
manufacturing, purchasing, projects and HR in one system. We recommend it to firms that make or move
physical things, where the real pain is not sales but the distance between the order, the warehouse
and the ledger. It is under exceptionally active development, which we like.

### Odoo — when the problem is going to change

Modular by design. A company can start with CRM alone and switch on invoicing, inventory or HR later
without changing systems or re-entering its data. We suggest it when a business knows it is going to
look different in two years but cannot yet say how. Its Community edition is genuinely capable, and
Hungarian localisation is well established.

Every system we run shares the two properties that made us pick them in the first place: an open
licence, and a standard database underneath. The choice between them is a conversation about your
business, not about software — which is why we would rather show you the whole range than sell you
one.
